What is Investing & the Stock Market?

Investing = buying assets like stocks, bonds, or funds that can grow in value or pay income over time. Buying one share of a company means you own a tiny slice of it. If the company grows, your slice is worth more.

What you'll learn

Stocks

  • Share: A unit of ownership in a company.
  • Dividend: A cash payment some companies make to shareholders from profits.
  • Market cap: A company's market value = share price × number of shares.

Index funds & ETFs

  • Index fund: A fund that tracks a market index, like the S&P 500, instead of picking stocks.
  • ETF: Exchange-traded fund: a basket of investments that trades like a stock.
  • Expense ratio: The yearly fee a fund charges, as a % of your investment.

Risk & diversification

  • Diversification: Owning many different investments so one failure has limited impact.
  • Asset allocation: How you split money between stocks, bonds, and cash.
  • Time horizon: How long until you need the money. Longer horizons can usually ride out more ups and downs.

Investor behavior

  • Market timing: Trying to jump in and out before ups and downs. Very hard to do consistently.
  • Dollar-cost averaging: Investing a fixed amount on a schedule, whatever the price.
  • FOMO: Fear of missing out: chasing hype after prices have already soared.
