What is Statistics?

Statistics = learning from data under uncertainty: summarizing it, finding patterns, and deciding how confident to be. A poll of 1,000 voters can estimate the views of millions, with a margin of error of about ±3 percentage points.

What you'll learn

Describing data

  • Mode: The most frequent value.
  • Standard deviation: How far values typically sit from the average. Bigger = more spread out.
  • Skew: When data has a long tail on one side that pulls the mean that way.

Probability

  • Probability: The chance of an event, between 0 and 1 (or 0% and 100%).
  • Independent events: One outcome doesn't affect the other, so you multiply their probabilities.
  • Gambler's fallacy: Believing past random results change future odds.

Sampling & bias

  • Random sample: Everyone in the population has an equal chance of being picked.
  • Sampling bias: When the sample doesn't represent the population.
  • Sample size: Bigger samples give more precise estimates, with diminishing returns.

Inference & significance

  • Hypothesis test: Checking whether a result is likely to be just random chance.
  • p-value: The probability of seeing a result at least this extreme if there were truly no effect.
  • Confidence interval: A range of plausible values for the true number.
